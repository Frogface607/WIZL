Version: 1.0
Date: July 21, 2026
Owner: Sergey
Market: English-first international audience

## Executive decision

Launch WIZL as a free cannabis field guide and pocket companion.

Do not launch it as:

- Shazam for weed
- a dispensary map
- a marketplace
- a paid AI scanner
- a shop-listing business
- a generic cannabis encyclopedia

The wedge is personal memory:

Read the label. Remember the experience.

The world of WIZL is the distribution and brand moat. The field-note habit is the product moat.

## Why this category can work

Large incumbents already own broad strain SEO, reviews, and local commerce. A new directory cannot out-Leafly Leafly or out-marketplace Weedmaps without capital, data provenance, and regulatory exposure.

Most journal apps solve memory but are visually generic. Most AI scanners overpromise identity. WIZL can occupy the gap:

A recognizable character world wrapped around a privacy-first personal taste journal and cautious label literacy.

The strategic asset stack is:

1. Original IP: WIZL, the cat, the Book, artifacts, locations, and serialized quests.
2. Structured reference corpus: more than 3,000 strain entries.
3. Personal taste graph: what an individual logged, noticed, and revisited.
4. Founder narrative: a real unfinished project returning to life.
5. Content engine: useful education plus repeatable adventures.

AI vision is useful infrastructure, not the moat.

## Beachhead customer

Primary segment:

Adults in legal markets who buy or receive labeled products, try different names, and later cannot remember which batch, aroma, or experience worked for them.

Trigger moments:

- standing with a package and wanting to decode it
- comparing a familiar name from a different producer
- forgetting what was enjoyable last month
- noticing tolerance or wanting to cut down
- wanting a private record without joining a social network

Job to be done:

When I encounter a labeled cannabis product, help me understand the clues and remember my own response so I can make a more conscious choice next time.

Anxieties:

- “Will this app expose or sell my cannabis data?”
- “Is the AI making this up?”
- “Is a strain name even meaningful?”
- “Do I need an account?”
- “Is this trying to sell me something?”
- “Will this glorify overuse?”

Desired outcome:

A fast, private, honest memory aid that feels friendly enough to keep.

## Payment reality

Current checkout must remain paused.

Why:

Major general processors explicitly restrict cannabis, related businesses, or age-restricted drug content. Gumroad’s prohibited-business policy lists marijuana dispensaries and related businesses and separately restricts many AI services. Stripe lists cannabis and related products among prohibited businesses. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy restrict age-regulated or drug-related categories.

A creator-membership platform may be a candidate for educational media support, but eligibility, founder location, payout access, content framing, and merchandising still require direct written approval.

Decision gate before collecting one dollar:

1. Send the provider a plain-English description of WIZL, sample content, screenshots, and intended benefits.
2. Ask for written approval for the exact business model.
3. Keep the approval record.
4. Add accurate renewal, cancellation, refund, tax, and identity disclosures.
5. Add server-side entitlement verification.
6. Never unlock paid status from a URL query or browser flag.

Sources:

- Gumroad prohibited products: https://gumroad.com/prohibited
- Stripe restricted businesses: https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses
- Paddle acceptable use: https://www.paddle.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy
- Lemon Squeezy acceptable use: https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/acceptable-use-policy
- Patreon community guidelines: https://www.patreon.com/policy/guidelines

## Forty-five-day launch

### Days 1 to 3: truth and release candidate

- finish product truth pass
- deploy and smoke-test production
- verify no old checkout, shop promise, account-sync promise, or visual-identification claim remains
- test twenty real labels and record every miss
- connect official social links only after handles are confirmed
- prepare analytics baseline and cost alert
- publish the Meet WIZL carousel if founder video is not approved

Exit gate:

A new adult understands WIZL, saves a field note, and knows data is local.

### Days 4 to 10: founder launch week

- publish Founder Episode 1 when approved
- publish product ritual
- publish THC Is Not The Whole Story
- publish one WIZL adventure
- publish one tolerance or moderation post
- answer every serious comment
- recruit five interview participants

Exit gate:

Twenty activated users or enough qualitative evidence to explain why activation is lower.

### Days 11 to 21: research sprint

- complete fifteen user conversations
- analyze twenty label-read cases
- tag feedback by trigger, anxiety, workaround, and desired outcome
- measure unknown rate and false-confidence reports
- ask activated users what would make them return
- do not build sync until users reveal the need

Exit gate:

One repeatable trigger and one clear retention problem.

### Days 22 to 35: retention sprint

Choose the smallest evidence-backed feature, likely one of:

- producer and batch fields
- faster repeat-log flow
- weekly personal recap
- shareable field-note card
- clearer taste-trail explanation
- structured break and tolerance notes

Ship one, instrument it, and compare return behavior.

Exit gate:

At least ten activated users return and save another note, or the team changes the wedge.

### Days 36 to 45: Bangkok preparation

- confirm current law with Thai counsel or a qualified local compliance professional
- create a licensed-partner verification checklist
- shortlist labs, educators, researchers, cultivators, and quality-focused licensed operators
- request filming and trademark permission in writing
- prepare a founder-return chapter with no venue promotion
- prepare travel safety, budget, and production schedule
- approach no partner with a public listing promise

Exit gate:

A compliant research plan and at least three qualified conversations, not three “featured shops.”

## Scientific trust basis

Named commercial strains can vary genetically and chemically, and common Indica/Sativa labels do not reliably map to a product’s full chemistry or a person’s experience. That is why WIZL must privilege the actual label, producer, batch, chemistry, and personal note over visual confidence.

Sources:

- Cannabis cultivar genetic identity: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815053/
- Commercial label inconsistency: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0267498
- Indica/Sativa label and chemistry analysis: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8516649/

## Competitor frame

Leafly and Weedmaps own broad discovery, reviews, SEO, and commerce. Tetragram and newer journals validate the private-tracking job. Quit and tolerance apps validate demand for reduction support.

WIZL should learn from them without copying their category:

- Leafly: corpus and search depth
- Weedmaps: local intent, but avoid its marketplace exposure
- Tetragram and STASH: journaling utility and privacy
- Grounded: tolerance and quitting support
- WIZL: character IP plus field-note habit

The launch question is not whether WIZL has more features. It is whether adults remember it, trust it, and write a second note.

## Decision rules

Build a feature when:

- at least five qualified users describe the same problem, or
- funnel data exposes a clear bottleneck, or
- it is required for truth, safety, cost control, or compliance

Do not build a feature because:

- a competitor has it
- it makes the landing page sound larger
- it creates a fake monetization story
- it depends on unverified venue or strain data
- it cannot be explained in one honest sentence
